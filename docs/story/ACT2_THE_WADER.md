# Act 2. Afterglow: the Wader

*Light ceiling 0.7. Outline only; nothing here is built.*

**In one line:** a colossus is walking into a sea that dissolves paint, to be clean of the town on its back, and you learn in its water what you are made of.

**The act's question:** what does it cost a thing to be finished? (Act 1 asked what it costs *you*.)

**Personal before cosmic:** the reveal that lands first is a seven-year-old girl who has been seven for nine years. The reveal about the Limner's own body comes after, and is quieter.

## The region

The Matriarch's child. Its people call it **Ford**. It woke cold, felt its back crawling, and is walking west into **the Thinner**: a still, clear sea that takes paint off anything it touches. It moves one stride a day and does not know anyone is there.

| Zone | State | What it is | Light |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lathe** (hub) | Painted, peeling | A town of scaffolds, rope lifts and stairs lashed to Ford's shoulders. It is taken down and re-lashed higher as the water rises. Nothing in Lathe is older than a season, and everyone can tie six knots. | 0.7 |
| **The Tideline** | Half-painted / Sketch by the hour | Ford's flanks between high and low water. The old lower town. Twice a day the Thinner uncovers its streets as bare drawing and then drowns them. | 0.4 dry, 0 under water |
| **Stillwater** | Set | Ansel Verdigris's terrace. Every board, cup and person set. Tide-proof, lamp-proof, perfect, and exactly as it was nine years ago. | Needs none |
| **The Sound** (dungeon) | Sketch, flooded | Inside Ford's chest, entered through the wash-holes behind its jaw. Half full of Thinner that rises and falls with its breath. | 0 |

**The region's shift** is the tide. Low water and high water are the A and B versions of the Tideline, and the player moves between them by resting. Low water opens the old streets and the way into the Sound. High water closes them and lifts the Skimmers up to Lathe's lower rungs.

**The Thinner as a rule.** Wading costs wet pigment: every few seconds in the water, one unit of something you carry wet is gone. Dry pigment is safe. So the sea turns the wet-and-dry rule from a death penalty into a route-planning question: what do I dare carry across?

**New here:** the third palette well; salvage ("slick", skimmed off the Thinner: cheap pigment that makes mud); set objects as terrain (they cannot be harvested, lit, moved or broken).

## Characters

**Tamsin Rill, tide-reader.** Forty, quick, always mid-task. Her grandmother's tide-book is her inheritance and it has been wrong for a year, because Ford is walking faster. She does not want Ford stopped: "It's not ours. We're on it." She wants her people off alive. Open, and means to stay so.
- *Wants:* a place to take two hundred people. The Matriarch is the only one in sight.
- *Flaw:* she moves the town so well that nobody believes they must leave it.

**Ansel Verdigris, Limner.** Sixty, gentle, certain. He trained in Varnish beside Odile and left when she laughed at him. He decided that leaving things open is cowardice dressed as respect, and set his own street: his wife, his neighbours, a child who was drowning.
- *Wants:* to set all of Lathe before the water takes it. He has the pigment for perhaps a third.
- *The tell:* nothing he made has ever needed mending, and he mentions it too often.
- *He is not a villain.* Every person in Stillwater is alive because of him. Ask them and they will say they are happy. They will say it in the same words each time.

**Pell.** Seven. Set at seven, nine years ago, in the moment Ansel pulled her out of the tide. She is delightful and she cannot learn your name: tomorrow she will ask it again. Her mother, **Oona**, is open, sixteen years older than the day it happened, and comes every morning to be met as a stranger by her own daughter.

**Nell Sedge, salvager.** Bartram's cousin. Skims slick off the Thinner and sells it by the ladle. Honest about what it is: "It's everybody's colour and nobody's. Makes a fine brown."

**The Understudy.** One of the lesser drafts that followed Prime out of the Vault. It has half a face. It does not want to be a copy of anyone and asks the only Limner it has met for a face of its own.

**Prime.** See `docs/STORY.md`. In this act it is courteous, lonely and persuasive. It has been looking for you for years. It does not attack you. It leaves you alone with what it said, which is worse.

## Quests

### Main

1. **The Long Stride.** From the Matriarch's nape you can see Ford's lamps. Cross at slack tide on Tamsin's rope ferry. Arrive in Lathe as it is being moved up a level around you. *Teaches:* the tide clock, set objects, the Thinner's cost.
2. **Low Water.** The way into Ford is through the drowned lower town. Read the tide, go down the Tideline while it is bare, and at the bottom there is no way round: you must wade the last channel. **You wade.** A layer comes off you. Tamsin, on the far rope, sees charcoal where everyone else shows white. The third palette well opens. Prime is waiting on the other bank.
3. **What the Water Wants.** The Sound. The boss is **Old Mud**: everything Ford has washed off itself, every colour run together. Its hue changes as you strike it, so the Contrast rule has to be read moment to moment. Then the wall of Ford's heart, and an answer:
   - **Set its heart.** Ford stops and kneels in the shallows, forever, with Lathe safe on its back. It never gets clean. Its eye stays open.
   - **Let it wash.** Ford walks on. Lathe must be carried to the Matriarch before the next tide; who you can save depends on what you did for Tamsin.
   - **Answer in its mother's colour.** Only if the Matriarch's Heart wall was painted in Act 1: paint Ford's wall the same. It stops, turns its head toward her, and wades back along the shore instead of out to sea. Nobody is set. Nobody is drowned. It costs more pigment than either other choice and it is the reason not to leave the first wall bare.

### Side

- **Nine Years of Tuesday** (Oona). She asks you to set her too, so that she and Pell will at least be the same day old. Do it; refuse; or do the third thing, which the game never lists: come back each in-game day and tell Pell your name again. After the fifth time Oona stops asking. There is no reward. Players will remember it.
- **Tide Tables** (Tamsin). Fetch her grandmother's book from the lower town at low water. Inside is the last correct table and a pressed flower from a street that no longer exists. Choice: set the town's **Riding Light** so that it can never go out (and can never be carried to a new level, so Lathe stays where the lamp is), or leave it open and help carry it.
- **The Understudy.** Give it a face. The game offers the features of people you have met: Hesper's brow, Corin's mouth, Odile's eyes if you found all her notes. Whatever you choose, it becomes someone new, keeps the lantern-hand, and can be taught the knife in Act 3.
- **Salvage** (Nell). Slick is cheap attunement. Each node bought with it works, and dulls the Contrast bonus by a little, permanently. "Mud's still paint."
- **The Green Door** (Ansel). He asks you to help him set the lower lifts before the spring tide. Helping saves forty people and fixes them. Refusing leaves them to Tamsin's ropes. Ansel never raises his voice either way.

## Key scenes

**Tamsin, first meeting** (she is hauling a rope and does not stop): "Limner? Good. Hold this. No, lower. Ford took a stride and a half last night and my book says one. Either the book's wrong or it's in a hurry, and my gran was never wrong."

**Oona, at Stillwater's gate:** "She'll like you. She likes everyone. She'll have forgotten by supper, so don't take it to heart. I've had nine years to stop taking it to heart and I'm nearly there."

**Pell:** "I'm seven! I've been seven for ages."

**Ansel, in his doorway:** "Odile used to say a thing left open can still surprise you. She was right. It can surprise you by drowning."

**The wading** (no dialogue). The water is clear and cold. The colour goes off your hands like breath off glass. Under it: lines. Not skin, not white ground. Careful, searching lines, some of them corrected. On the far bank Tamsin has stopped hauling.

**Tamsin, after:** "I'm not going to ask. I've two hundred people to move. But I saw."

**Prime, at the low-tide line:** "Third. I wondered what you'd made of yourself. You've made yourself useful. That's what I did, at first."
> "We were tried and put aside. That is all a study is. It kept the page, because the Hand never threw anything away, but it did not keep *us*."
> "I am not angry. I am tired of watching them come apart, and so are you."

**Ford's heart wall, confirmation text:** "This cannot be painted over. It will carry it for as long as it lives. Paint it?"

## What Act 1 choices do here

| Act 1 | Act 2 |
|---|---|
| Matriarch's wall left bare | She has woken further and follows Ford. Varnish is travelling. The third answer at Ford's wall is closed. |
| Wall painted Sanguine or Terre Verte | The third answer is open, in that colour. |
| Corin set as warden or gatekeeper | He greets Tamsin's refugees kindly and cannot be made to understand why they are afraid. |
| Corin left open | He comes with you as far as the nape, sees Ford's back from behind, and laughs for the first time in the game: "Look at that. Nobody did his either." |
| The Hem whitewashed with Ceruse | The Hem's children are ill. Bartram has stopped selling. |
| Odile's flame poured into the Lampstand | Varnish's light counts 0.1 higher for the whole act. Hesper has nothing of Odile's in her hands. |
| Odile's flame given to Hesper | Hesper carries it, and comes to the nape to see you off. She is at risk for the rest of the game, and she is who can finish you. |

# The cast in the game

Written 2026-11-19 by Claude (Fable), as a proposal. Nine figures from Krish and Hannah Sanghvi's folded-paper
drawings, cast into Underpainting. The drawings and their personalities come from `docs/CAST.md` in the drawings
repository; the films' story does not come with them. The owner's advisor's starting ideas are kept where they
fitted and changed where the world offered something better. Everything here is open to being redrawn.

**Credit: Characters by Krish & Hannah Sanghvi.**

## Who they are in this world: the Folded

The story already says why the Hand left the world unfinished: *company you have finished yourself is only
yourself again. A set thing cannot surprise you.* (`docs/STORY.md`, section 3.)

Before the Hand learned to leave things open, it tried a simpler trick to surprise itself. It folded the sheet.
It drew a head, folded it out of sight, drew a body without looking, folded again, drew legs. Then it opened the
paper and met someone it had not planned. These are **the Folded**: the Hand's first company, older than
people, made in a game it played alone.

That is exactly how the real drawings were made, which is why they belong here without any bending.

What follows from it, and what makes them different from everyone else in the game:

- **They are ink, not paint.** Ink does not need light to hold. The Folded do not fade in the Dim, so they live
  where people cannot: the forest's shade, the Vault, the edges. In a game about light running out, they are the
  ones who are not afraid of the dark.
- **They have seams.** Each is three ideas that never saw each other. They do not always agree with themselves:
  hands that drift off, heads that argue, legs that go another way. Their comedy and their sadness are the same thing.
- **Ink can be rubbed out.** An Erasure cannot fade a Fold, but it can scrape a part of one away. That is what
  happened to Lacuna's face.
- **They were never finished and never left open. They were just drawn.** So the game's central question lands
  on them sideways. Setting one makes it one thing at last: it stops arguing with itself, and stops surprising anyone.
- **How they look in the game:** black line kept exactly as drawn, with thin Glaze colour inside it, and a black
  edge all round. Among the painted villagers they should read as drawings that got up and walked.

People in Varnish call them margin-folk. Nobody is sure whether meeting one is luck.

### d40 Lacuna (the Faceless One). In the slice now.

A *lacuna* is what picture restorers call a place where the paint is lost. The Hem's children call them Lac.

**Who.** Lacuna keeps the water in the Hem, the lowest terrace of Varnish: the cistern is their middle, the
pipes are their legs, the taps drip when they are sad, and they are sad most of the time, gently. Their hands
float a little way off and do the work. Where a face should be there is bare canvas, cross-hatched, the same
bare canvas the game already uses for anything unpainted. An Erasure took the face years ago. Lacuna does not
speak, having nothing to speak with. They answer with their hands, and the game writes down what the hands do.

**What you do.** A side story, *A Face for Lacuna*, open after the first quest. You find them by the wall in
the Hem. One hand goes to the place where the face was, and stops. You can:

- **Finish the face.** It costs 3 Hide Ochre, and gives them a plain warm face, like a small moon. They are set:
  one person, for good. The taps stop. They stand straighter and the hands come home to the sleeves. They will
  never be afraid of the dark again, and they will never again be anyone but this. (As built, one colour. Letting
  the colour you spend decide the face is the natural next step.)
- **Leave it open.** Nothing is spent. A hand closes over yours for a moment. The taps keep dripping. They
  are still missing something, and still free.
- Walk away, and come back.

It is the game's question at the size of one neighbour, and it comes *before* the Heart wall, so the player has
practised the choice once with low stakes before making it with high ones.

**Why it matters later (proposal).** The Finale needs "somebody else to hold the brush for the side you cannot
see", and only someone left open can. Lacuna has two free hands and no face of their own to be vain about. If
left open, and kept in the light, Lacuna is one of the people who can finish you. If set, they cannot: they
will only stand and look, with the face you gave them.

**Changed from the advisor's idea:** kept whole. Added the name, the job, the hands that talk, and the Finale.

**Built (devlog 015):** in the Hem under the wall of the square, in both versions of the village. Side story
`SQ_AFaceForLacuna`, eight lines in `data/dialogue.json`, decided at the bare place itself.

**Moves like (from CAST.md):** glides upright while the pipe frame shuffles like a parallelogram on curl-toed
slippers; the hands drift round like satellites; slow, deliberate, dripping.

### d28 the Hatcher, and Stipple (the Spider). In the slice now.

*Hatching* is shading with drawn lines. The Hatcher's web is the cross-hatching the forest's shadows are made
of. She is not a villain, she is a worker: patient, unhurried, proprietary, and protective of the small dotted
one who rides on her back.

**Where.** The Bend in the Furrow, over the far corner: the corner with the cache in it.

**How she works.** Her web is strung across that corner and **cannot be seen in the half-light**. Light shows
it: a Flare, or a lit Lampstand's glow, and every strand stands out as a drawn line. Walk into a strand you
did not see and you are held for a moment, and she comes down her thread and does everything at once. See the
web first and you can step between the strands, take the cache, and leave her hanging there, watching. She
never attacks anyone who has not touched her web.

So she is a trap you can read, not an ambush: the first place outside the Vault where the Flare is worth its
Glim, and a reason to look before you walk. Killing her is possible and pays in Bone Black; it is not required.

**Hue.** Black. Ink is weak to its opposite, white: Ceruse hurts her most, Bone Black least.

**Built (devlog 015):** over the corner cache at the Bend, in both versions of the forest, and alone in the test
room `Maps/Dev/L_Combat_Hatcher`. Enemy row `Hatcher`. Stipple is not made yet.

**Changed from the advisor's idea:** a creature with one place and one rule, not a mini-boss. A boss here
would have been the forest's third peak in a row. Stipple is saved for later: if the Hatcher dies, the small
one is left on the thread, and someone in Act 2 could ask after it.

**Moves like (from CAST.md):** comes down and goes up her thread, turning slowly; walks four legs at a time
with slow placing and a quick last tap of each foot; then a sudden rush when something is caught. Curls all
eight legs under her as a bluff.

### d24 Piecemeal (the Summoned). Slice, next.

A body that is never finished: a hand with an eye for a head, a chest that is a swarm of jigsaw pieces, legs
that are smoke tied to two oil lamps. It can only be as far as its smoke reaches.

**The fit.** Two Lampstands in Act 1 are already burning when you arrive, and nothing in the game says why.
Piecemeal is why. It keeps the last Lampstand in the Vault, on the ledge before the Heart, because its own two
lamps are lit from it and it cannot leave. It watched Odile walk past eleven years ago. It gives pieces of
itself away (it is generous) and is always one piece short. It scatters when startled and assembles to be seen.

**What you do.** Bring it a Graphite Shard and it presses the shard into its chest as the missing piece; it does
not fit, but it keeps it. In return it shows what it has been hoarding beside the lamp. A small trade, a small
sadness, and a keeper for a safe place.

### d05 the Vigil (the Mother). Slice, later.

Eyes held wide apart on stalks so she can look two ways at once; a belly like a lantern; she walks on two
sleeping heads. Grave, slow, protective.

**The fit.** She stands in the doorway of the Heart and does not come in. One eye is on the hollow, where a
woman has been keeping a heart warm for eleven years. The other is on the stair, for whoever comes next. She is
the only witness there has been. After the fight, both eyes turn to the hollow, and then, for the first time, one
turns to look at you. She says nothing and is not a fight. After the Turning she is at the Nape Overlook
instead, one eye on the Matriarch, the other on the Wader walking west: a mother watching a mother watch a child.

**To settle with the owner:** the drawing has bare breasts and an unborn child in a cutaway. CAST.md flags it
too. My suggestion is to keep the lantern-belly as light behind a closed oval and dress the figure. This one
needs the owner's eye before it is made.

### d25 the Unpinned (the Darter). Act 2.

A specimen that has come off its pin: a beetle's head, a dragonfly's wings, one bird leg folded like a heron's,
ending in a blade. It stands perfectly still for a very long time and then moves once.

**The fit.** The enemy that teaches patience back at you. It does not chase. It perches on a scaffold pole or a
rib, turning its head in small clicks, and darts in a straight line with a hard stop, when you move. Stand
still and it loses you. Its fear is the one it was made from: a Fixative Flask pins it in place. It hunts the
Hatcher's kind and cuts strands, so where both live a clever player can lead one into the other. Act 2's
scaffolds are the right home (straight lines, long drops). One could stand on the Stair of Spines after the
Turning as a first sight.

### d01 the Larder (the Icebox). Act 2, or Varnish later.

A cold cabinet of kept things under eight heads that whisper to each other, rooted on a serpent's tail.
Possessive, sly, a gossip. It opens its door a crack at a time.

**The fit, and a change.** The advisor said a hoarding pigment trader. The game has a rule that makes this
better than a shop: **wet pigment is lost when you die, and only resting dries it.** The Larder keeps things
cold, and cold paint does not dry and does not spoil. Leave your wet pigment in the Larder and it is safe from
dying, and stays wet; take it out later for a share. A bank that takes a cut, run by something that would
rather you never came back for it. The heads pay in rumour: each tells you where one hidden thing is, and
they do not agree. The door's cold light is the only light it has, and it only shines while the door is open.

### d27 the Quarrel. Act 2.

Two shouting heads joined at the hair, a body that is only legs pulling five ways, a tree trunk rooted in one
great foot. A small animal clings to the trunk and is the one calm thing it has.

**The fit.** Ansel Verdigris's perfect terrace "has not had a new song, child or argument in nine years". The
Quarrel is the argument. It was moved outside the gate. One head wants to be set, to have the shouting over;
the other would rather shout forever than be one thing. Both are the same creature, so whichever you help, you
are doing it to the other as well. The third way is the animal: tend the calm thing and the heads tire and
lean on each other. It is Act 2's theme (what finishing costs the finished) with nobody else to blame.

### d29 the Lineman and the Keep (the Pole and the Keep). Act 2.

A pole-headed figure in a suit, walking on its hands, leading by one arm a ragged companion whose legs are
castle towers. In one tower's shin is a lit window where someone is waiting. Crows sit on the Pole's wires.

**The fit.** Kept as the advisor had it: **a Lampstand that walks.** The Keep's window is a place to rest, and
it is wherever the two of them have got to. In Act 2 the tide moves the streets twice a day, so a rest point
that moves is not a gimmick, it is the terrain. You learn their route. The crows fly up when anything hostile
is near, which makes the Pole a warning you can read from far off. They cannot let go of each other, and
nobody in the game asks them to.

### d21 the Lay Hand (the Puppeteer). Act 3, in reserve.

A head of tentacles whose neck is an arm, whose hand works a wooden mannequin on strings. Serene, hypnotic.
She never touches the ground. Everything below her hand is dangled, not owned.

**The fit.** A painter's studio has a wooden jointed figure for working out poses: a lay figure. In the Studio
at the edge of the picture, the Hand's own lay figure is still there, and so is what holds its strings. It is
the figure the three self-portrait studies were posed from, which makes it the oldest version of you. The
fight is against the puppet while she floats out of reach; her strings, like the Hatcher's web, only show in
light, and a string that is lit can be cut. She comes before Prime, and she is the same argument in a
different voice: Prime wants everything fixed in place, she already holds something that is.

Act 2 has its boss (Old Mud). This is not needed before Act 3 and should wait.

## What enters Act 1 now, and what it costs

- **Lacuna:** one person, one short side story, three ways to end it, no fighting. In the Hem, which the main
  path does not pass through, so nobody is slowed down.
- **The Hatcher:** one creature in one corner, off the main path, that never attacks first.

Neither changes the main quest, the pacing table, or the balance of the path. Everything else on this page is
paper until the owner says otherwise.
